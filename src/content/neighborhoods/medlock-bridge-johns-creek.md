## Medlock Bridge: The Residential Half of Johns Creek

Medlock Bridge takes its name from William Oliver Medlock, who ran a ferry and then a metal bridge across the Chattahoochee here in the late 1800s. What grew up around the crossing is now one of Johns Creek's largest residential communities: a run of HOA-governed subdivisions along Medlock Bridge Road and Medlock Bridge Parkway, built mostly between the 1980s and the 2000s, in brick and siding, on generous lots.

### What Drives Demand

The school catchment does most of the work. Medlock Bridge Elementary School opened at 10215 Medlock Bridge Parkway in April 1990 and the community filled in around it; the area also feeds Johns Creek High School. Families move here for that and they stay, which is why the letting market is a household market rather than a nightly one.

The second driver is employment a short drive away. Technology Park holds Alcon — the city's largest employer at 1,836 people — along with Emory Johns Creek Hospital at 1,200 and Macy's Systems & Technology at 997. A transferee arriving for one of those jobs with school-age children is looking at exactly this catchment, and often needs somewhere furnished for two or three months before they buy.

### The Rules That Apply

Johns Creek does not license short-term rentals; its zoning code simply defines a Dwelling as residential use "for periods of more than 30 consecutive days," which puts nightly letting outside the permitted use in residential districts. On top of the city code sit the individual subdivision covenants, several of which set their own minimum lease terms and tenant-approval steps. Both get checked for the specific address before we quote anything.

### How We Manage It

For most Medlock Bridge houses the honest answer is a twelve-month lease with a tenant screened against a written income standard and a documented move-in condition report — the things that decide whether a deposit dispute goes your way two years later. Where a furnished thirty-day-plus let genuinely earns more, usually because the house sits well for a Technology Park relocation, we will say so and set it up that way instead.
