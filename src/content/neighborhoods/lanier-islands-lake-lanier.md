## Lanier Islands: The Resort End of the Lake

Lanier Islands is not a town and not a neighbourhood. It is a former chain of islands — a connected peninsula since the lake was impounded — held on lease from the US Army Corps of Engineers and run as one integrated resort under the Margaritaville brand. The Corps' own Shoreline Management Plan names the Lanier Islands complex as one of only eleven sites on the entire lake authorised for commercial activity.

### What That Means for an Owner

The lodging inside the resort is the resort's: hotel rooms, waterfront cabins, glamping tents and RV pitches at Camp Margaritaville. There is no meaningful private short-term rental inventory on the leased land itself. The opportunity is on genuinely private shoreline nearby, letting into demand this end of the lake creates rather than competing with the resort's own rooms.

### The Demand It Creates

Wild Waves Water Park, billed as home to Georgia's largest wave pool, brings day-trippers up from Atlanta who regularly decide to stay. Lanier Islands Beach offers roughly half a mile of sand swim beach, which is genuinely unusual on a reservoir and matters enormously to families with young children. The Peachtree Pointe Amphitheater runs a summer concert series, the resort programmes three nights of fireworks around the Fourth of July, and the lodge takes weddings and conferences that spill beyond its own capacity.

### The Two Rules That Decide Everything

First, the dock. A Corps permit runs with ownership and not occupancy, cannot be held by someone renting the property, cannot have its moorage sublet, and becomes void on sale or transfer. With a lakewide cap of 10,615 permits set in the Corps' plan, a dock is something you buy with the house or plan without.

Second, the county. This shoreline is Forsyth County, whose ordinance is reported to confine short-term rentals to agricultural and agricultural-residential zoning with a conditional use permit — a materially stricter position than Hall County across the water. We could not read Forsyth's ordinance directly and say so plainly; it is the first thing we confirm on a specific address.

### How We Manage It

We price to the resort's calendar rather than to a flat nightly rate, because the water park season, the concert series and the Fourth of July weekend are the three things that move the number here. And we settle the zoning and dock questions before a property is listed, not after.
